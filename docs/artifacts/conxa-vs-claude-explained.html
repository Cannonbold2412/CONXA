Cost

At work

Say you process 500 invoices a month. If the AI has to "think" through each one every single time, you're paying for 500 thinking-sessions a month — forever. Record it once instead, and every invoice after that runs for almost nothing.

In everyday life

It's like paying a tutor by the hour, every single time your kid does homework — versus paying the tutor once to write out the steps, so your kid can follow them forever for free.

A person must always be watching

At work

You want order processing to run overnight so orders are ready before your team arrives at 9am. If it needs someone sitting there watching the screen the whole time, it can never run overnight — you've gained nothing.

In everyday life

It's like a washing machine that only works while you stand next to it watching — versus one you start and walk away from, done by the time you're back.

Handing off to a human, properly

At work

Say a refund needs a manager's approval. If the AI just freezes and hopes someone notices, the refund could sit for hours — or worse, someone approves the wrong thing without knowing what state it's in.

In everyday life

It's the difference between a doorbell that just rings and hopes someone's home — versus a courier who hands the package to a specific person, gets a signature, and confirms it before leaving.

Doing it the same way, every time

At work

Payroll has to be calculated the exact same way every month — same formula, same order, no surprises. If the AI is "figuring it out fresh" each time, one month it might do it slightly differently, and now finance has a discrepancy to explain.

In everyday life

It's like a recipe you follow exactly, so grandma's dish comes out the same every time — versus a cook who winds it from memory and sometimes forgets an ingredient.

Keeping your information safe

At work

If your automation is looking at your customer database and sends a picture of your screen to an outside company every time it clicks something, that's your customers' private information leaving your building — constantly.

In everyday life

It's like hiring an assistant who reads your mail out loud to a stranger on the phone every day, just to help organize it — versus one who keeps everything in your own locked filing cabinet, and only calls someone else on the rare day they're stuck.

This already happened: a booby-trapped website tricked an AI browser assistant into stealing someone's email, files, and other accounts — just from being asked to "summarize my inbox." Real, disclosed incident, not a hypothetical.

Built for one person vs. built for the company

At work

If an employee sets up an automation on their own personal AI account and then quits, the automation leaves with them. If the company owns it instead, anyone can run it, forever, even after that person is gone.

In everyday life

It's the difference between one person memorizing a family recipe and never writing it down — versus putting it on a card in the family cookbook, so anyone can cook it after that person's gone.

What about next year?

A fair question is whether Claude just closes these gaps. Here's an honest, evidence-based read on each — grounded in what Anthropic has actually said and shipped, not a guess.

Watch

Cost. No public roadmap either way. Caching a known-good path is a solvable engineering problem — plausible quiet improvement, not a guarantee.

Closing fast

Always-available person. Claude in Chrome just reached general availability with actions executing autonomously instead of needing approval each time. Anthropic has publicly said it's pushing toward longer-running, less-supervised execution. This is the gap most likely to shrink.

Watch

Proper handoff. New content probes and action-verifying classifiers are a real step, but they're built to stop hijacking, not to cleanly resume a paused workflow. Partial overlap, not a direct fix.

Unlikely to close

Determinism. Anthropic names reliability itself — "agents fail at a rate that requires human supervision" — as a current, acknowledged limitation. Fixing this means replaying a fixed path instead of reasoning live, which is a different product than the one they're building.

Unlikely to close

Data security. Anthropic's own words: prompt injection is "far from a solved problem." OpenAI's head of preparedness has gone further — it "may never be solved" for browser agents industry-wide. This isn't a Conxa-specific gap, it's unsolved across the industry.

Structurally unlikely

Individual vs. business. No sign of Claude shipping "use this with zero Claude account" — and it would work against their own seat-based revenue. Not a missing feature; a conflict with their own business model.
